# Methods Summary

## 1. Data Collection

The project is structured around political discourse data from media and/or social platforms. Relevant data sources may include news coverage, social media posts, comments, transcripts, or other public-facing political texts.

Potential fields include:

- source,
- date/time,
- title or headline,
- text body,
- URL,
- platform,
- keyword,
- sentiment score,
- stance label,
- frame label.

## 2. Text Preparation

The text-as-data workflow may include:

- removing duplicate records,
- cleaning URLs and metadata,
- normalizing dates,
- filtering by keyword/topic relevance,
- lowercasing and whitespace cleanup,
- preparing text for sentiment, stance, or framing analysis.

## 3. Sentiment and Stance Measurement

The project can be analyzed using sentiment and stance labels to distinguish:

- positive, negative, or neutral tone,
- support, opposition, or neutrality toward actors or claims,
- shifts in evaluative language across sources or time.

## 4. Framing / Narrative Analysis

Political framing analysis focuses on how the event is interpreted rather than only whether language is positive or negative. Possible frame categories include:

- diplomacy/conflict,
- corruption/accountability,
- foreign aid,
- election politics,
- national security,
- institutional trust,
- leadership/personality framing.

## 5. Visualization and Reporting

Potential outputs include:

- time-series plots of volume and sentiment,
- source/platform comparisons,
- stance distributions,
- frame-frequency tables,
- keyword/topic summaries,
- narrative interpretation memo.

## Methodological Caution

Digital trace and media data are not representative samples of the public. The analysis should be interpreted as observed discourse, not population opinion. Source selection, platform incentives, and engagement dynamics can all shape the observed data.
